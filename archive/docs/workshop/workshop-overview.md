# Workshop Overview

## Purpose

Turn the initial accommodation-booking proposition into a shared, testable
definition of the problem, the first release, the operating model, and the
solution boundaries.

This is a decision workshop, not a design presentation. The architecture is a
working hypothesis and should change when business or product evidence requires
it.

## Participants

| Group | Contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Business owners | Outcomes, funding, policy intent, risk appetite, and ownership |
| Product owners | User needs, journeys, scope, prioritisation, and acceptance |
| Operations | Approval, booking, exception, support, and reconciliation processes |
| Technology | Feasibility, integration, data, security, quality, and delivery |
| Finance and procurement | Payment, merchant, tax, invoicing, and supplier terms |
| Security and privacy | Identity, access, personal data, audit, and compliance |

## Recommended workshop sequence

1. Align on the problem and intended outcomes.
2. Agree scope, users, journeys, policies, and the MVP.
3. Challenge the solution and Booking.com integration hypotheses.
4. Establish the required security, quality, and operational controls.
5. Resolve or assign decisions, risks, dependencies, and next steps.

## Working agreements

- Separate facts, assumptions, recommendations, and decisions.
- Name one accountable owner and a due date for every open decision.
- Use parking lots for detail that does not change scope or feasibility.
- Do not treat request approval as a confirmed hotel reservation.
- Record changes in the decision log before closing the workshop.

## Definition of done

The workshop is complete when participants have agreed an MVP boundary,
identified the discovery gates, assigned every blocking decision, and accepted
the next validation and delivery steps.
