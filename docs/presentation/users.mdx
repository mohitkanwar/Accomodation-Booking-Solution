# Entities

The accommodation ecosystem supports multiple client corporations and multiple
provider corporations. Every corporation retains its own users and
organisational boundary, while Sodexo coordinates the booking interaction.

# Users

The accommodation capability serves five user roles within a corporate travel
programme. Expand a role to review its associated user journey.

:::info Corporate policy context

A corporate is the client organisation rather than a user. It owns a travel
policy that defines role eligibility, permitted destinations, and price ranges
for its travellers, approvers, administrators, and booking operators.

:::

## User roles

<ExpandableRoleCard
  index="01"
  name="Traveller"
  description="A corporate employee who searches for accommodation, submits requests, and tracks their outcome."
>

1. Sign in through the existing Sodexo B2C application.
2. Select a permitted office destination, dates, and accommodation requirements.
3. Search, filter, and compare policy-evaluated Booking.com proposals.
4. Review the price, cancellation conditions, and policy explanation.
5. Select an option and submit an accommodation request.
6. Respond to clarification requests and track approval and booking statuses.
7. Receive the confirmed reservation or a clear rejection or failure outcome.

</ExpandableRoleCard>

<ExpandableRoleCard
  index="02"
  name="Approver"
  description="A traveller with additional authority to approve or reject requests from eligible subordinates."
>

1. Complete the traveller journey when booking accommodation for their own trip.
2. Open the queue of requests submitted by authorised subordinates.
3. Review the traveller, destination, dates, selected offer, price, and applicable policy.
4. Request clarification when the business purpose or exception evidence is incomplete.
5. Approve or reject the request with a reason.
6. Follow the downstream booking status without being able to approve their own request.
7. Retain a traceable record of the decision and supporting information.

</ExpandableRoleCard>

<ExpandableRoleCard
  index="03"
  name="Corporate Administrator"
  description="A client-side administrator who maintains travel policies, destinations, role levels, and price ranges."
>

1. Open the administration area within their authorised corporate scope.
2. Maintain corporate sites and the destinations available to travellers.
3. Define employee role levels and their destination-specific nightly price ranges.
4. Configure eligibility, exception, and approval rules.
5. Review the effective policy before publishing a new version.
6. Activate the policy from an agreed date without rewriting historical requests.
7. Review configuration history and correct or retire outdated rules.

</ExpandableRoleCard>

<ExpandableRoleCard
  index="04"
  name="Booking Operator"
  description="A service user who reviews approved requests and creates the real accommodation reservation."
>

1. Open the queue of requests assigned to the operator's corporate or destination scope.
2. Validate the request, approval evidence, traveller details, and policy outcome.
3. Recheck live Booking.com price, availability, and cancellation conditions.
4. Return the request for renewed approval if a material condition has changed.
5. Preview and create the Booking.com order through the controlled server-side workflow.
6. Record the supplier reference and confirm the reservation to the traveller.
7. Investigate failed or uncertain outcomes before retrying to avoid duplicate bookings.

The operator may also create a request on behalf of an authorised traveller,
with the delegated action recorded in the audit history.

</ExpandableRoleCard>

<ExpandableRoleCard
  index="05"
  name="Sodexo Administrator"
  description="A platform administrator who manages corporates, users, system configuration, and operational reporting."
>

1. Onboard or update a corporate tenant and its core configuration.
2. Assign appropriately scoped corporate, operator, and Sodexo administrative access.
3. Maintain platform-level integration endpoints and non-secret configuration.
4. Monitor configuration health, failed workflows, and reconciliation exceptions.
5. Generate operational, adoption, policy-compliance, and audit reports.
6. Support controlled investigation without silently impersonating business users.
7. Record every privileged change and administrative action for audit.

</ExpandableRoleCard>

## Cross-role controls

- Corporate and assignment scope must come from trusted server-side identity
  context, not editable browser fields.
- Approvers cannot approve their own requests.
- Policy and approval decisions must be preserved as immutable snapshots.
- On-behalf-of actions must identify both the actor and the traveller.
- Privileged configuration, decisions, and booking actions require audit
  evidence.
